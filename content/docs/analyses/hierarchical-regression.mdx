## When to use it

Use hierarchical regression when you, not an algorithm, decide the order. For example: enter study hours first, then ask whether anxiety still explains exam score after hours are already in the model.

## Assumptions

Same straight-line model as [linear regression](/docs/analyses/linear-regression). Tensr does not add residual plots or a collinearity table on this procedure.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Regression → Linear → Hierarchical. In chat: “Hierarchical regression of score, hours in block 1, hours and anxiety in block 2.”</Step>
<Step>Dependent is the outcome. Blocks is a list of predictor lists, at least one block.</Step>
<Step>Method is enter only. Confidence level starts at 0.95.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Same 24 students. Block 1 is `hours`. Block 2 is `hours` and `anxiety`. Outcome `score`.

| Block | Predictors | R² | Adj R² | ΔR² | F-change | p-change |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | hours | 0.965 | 0.964 | — | — | — |
| 2 | hours, anxiety | 0.988 | 0.987 | 0.023 | 20.035 | < .001 |

The first block has no previous model, so change in R², F-change, and p-change are em dashes. ΔR² on block 2 is how much R² rose when anxiety was added. p-change < .001 means that rise is not a plausible zero.

Final model coefficients:

| Variable | B | SE | β | 95% CI | p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| hours | 3.367 | 0.348 | 0.612 | [2.644, 4.09] | < .001 |
| anxiety | -1.97 | 0.311 | -0.4 | [-2.618, -1.323] | < .001 |

B is the raw slope. SE is its standard error. β is the standardized slope. Metrics are Final R² 0.988, Adj R² 0.987, and N 24.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Study hours alone accounted for R² = .97 of exam-score variance (N = 24). Adding anxiety increased R² by .02, F-change = 20.04, p < .001. In the final model, B = 3.37 for hours, 95% CI [2.64, 4.09], and B = −1.97 for anxiety, 95% CI [−2.62, −1.32].

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Regression → Linear → Hierarchical.

SPSS hierarchical regression is the Block button in Linear Regression: you assign predictors to Block 1 of 1, then the next block. Tensr takes those blocks as a list.
