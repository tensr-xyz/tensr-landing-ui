---
title: Friedman test
description: Compare three or more repeated measurements by their ranks.
category: Non-parametric
---

## When to use it

Use Friedman instead of a [repeated-measures ANOVA](/docs/analyses/anova-repeated) when the same people are measured three or more times and the scores are ordinal or skewed.

## Assumptions

Each row is one person. You need at least three measure columns. Kendall’s W is a separate analysis, not a field on this request.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → K Related Samples. In chat: “Friedman test of t1, t2, and t3.”</Step>
<Step>Select at least three measure columns.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Eight people, three occasions. Means were 5, 7, and 8.5.

| Measure | Mean |
| --- | ---: |
| t1 | 5 |
| t2 | 7 |
| t3 | 8.5 |

| χ² | df | p | Kendall's W |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 15 | 2 | < .001 | 0.938 |

The ranks differ across the three occasions. W = 0.938 is labeled large. The scale shared with Kendall’s W is: below 0.1 is negligible, 0.1 ≤ W < 0.3 is small, 0.3 ≤ W < 0.5 is moderate, and W ≥ 0.5 is large. Cutoffs: Tomczak & Tomczak, 2014.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A Friedman test showed that scores changed across the three occasions, χ²(2, N = 8) = 15.00, p < .001, W = .94.
