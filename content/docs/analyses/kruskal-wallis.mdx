## When to use it

Use Kruskal–Wallis instead of a [one-way ANOVA](/docs/analyses/anova-oneway) when you have three or more groups and the scores are ordinal or skewed.

## Assumptions

Groups are independent. The outcome is at least ordinal. The test does not tell you which groups differ until you turn on post hoc.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → K Independent Samples. In chat: “Kruskal–Wallis of score by method.”</Step>
<Step>Group column and value column are required. Post hoc starts off.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Six people in lecture, online, and workshop. Scores rise across those three labels.

| Group | n | Median |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lecture | 6 | 66.5 |
| Online | 6 | 73.5 |
| Workshop | 6 | 79.5 |

H = 15.158, p < .001. Epsilon-squared is 0.892, labeled large. Below 0.01 is negligible, 0.01 ≤ ε² < 0.08 is small, 0.08 ≤ ε² < 0.26 is medium, and ε² ≥ 0.26 is large. Cutoffs: Mangiafico, 2016. With post hoc on, Tensr adds pairwise Mann–Whitney tests and stores `p-adj`.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A Kruskal–Wallis test showed that scores differed by teaching method, H(2) = 15.16, p < .001, ε² = .89.
