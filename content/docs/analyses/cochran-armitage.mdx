---
title: Cochran–Armitage trend test
description: Test a linear trend in proportions across ordered groups.
category: Categorical
---

## When to use it

Use Cochran–Armitage when the outcome is binary and the groups have an order. It is the z-test cousin of the Mantel–Haenszel trend test.

## Assumptions

The group column must sort into the intended order. The outcome has two levels.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Correlate → Cochran-Armitage Trend. In chat: “Cochran–Armitage trend of dose by illness.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is ordered. Outcome column is binary.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Same twelve-person dose and illness table.

| z | Two-tailed p | Direction |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 4.899 | < .001 | increasing |

The stored direction is increasing, and the two-tailed p is below .001. One-tailed p is also stored. On this small table the z is large because the counts line up almost perfectly. Read it as a demonstration of the statistic, not as a stable effect size.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A Cochran–Armitage test showed an increasing trend in illness across dose, z = 4.90, p < .001.
