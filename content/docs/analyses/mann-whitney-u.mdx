## When to use it

Use Mann–Whitney U instead of an [independent-samples t-test](/docs/analyses/ttest-independent) when the outcome is ordinal or the scores are skewed. It asks whether one group tends to rank higher than the other.

## Assumptions

The two groups are independent. The outcome is at least ordinal. Tensr does not run a normality check for this test.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → 2 Independent Samples. In chat: “Mann–Whitney U of score by method.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is the two groups. Value column is the score.</Step>
<Step>Confidence level starts at 0.95. There is no effect-size option on this request.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Eight lecture scores and eight workshop scores, with a little overlap.

| U | p | n |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 2.500 | .002 | 16 |

U = 2.5 is small, which means the ranks barely overlap. p = .002, so the workshop scores tend to rank higher. The result stores the group names and sizes. It does not store a Wilcoxon W or a z on this run. The rank-biserial r is labeled with Cohen’s correlation benchmarks: |r| below 0.1 is negligible, 0.1 ≤ |r| < 0.3 is small, 0.3 ≤ |r| < 0.5 is medium, and |r| ≥ 0.5 is large. A value exactly on a cutoff takes the higher label. Cutoffs: Cohen, 1988.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Workshop scores ranked higher than lecture scores, U = 2.50, p = .002.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → 2 Independent Samples. SPSS often prints Wilcoxon W and a z alongside U.
