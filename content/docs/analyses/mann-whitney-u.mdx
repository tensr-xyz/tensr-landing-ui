## When to use it

Use Mann–Whitney U instead of an [independent-samples t-test](/docs/analyses/ttest-independent) when the outcome is ordinal or the scores are skewed. It asks whether one group tends to rank higher than the other.

## Assumptions

The two groups are independent. The outcome is at least ordinal. Tensr does not run a normality check for this test.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → 2 Independent Samples. In chat: “Mann–Whitney U of score by method.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is the two groups. Value column is the score.</Step>
<Step>Confidence level starts at 0.95. There is no effect-size option on this request.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Lecture versus workshop ranks on the exam. The workshop scores tend to sit higher, and the two lists still overlap, so U is not zero.

| Group | n | Median | Mean rank | Rank sum | Status |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Lecture | 32 | 69.1 | 26.312 | 842 | included |
| Workshop | 32 | 72.95 | 38.688 | 1,238 | included |

Significant difference in `score` between Workshop and Lecture (U = 314, p = .008, r = 0.332). Effect size (medium; r < 0.1 negligible, 0.1 ≤ r < 0.3 small, 0.3 ≤ r < 0.5 medium, r ≥ 0.5 large). Cutoffs: Cohen, 1988. The primary result is significant (p = .008). This is large enough, in this sample, to treat the comparison this page is about as a real association rather than noise.
