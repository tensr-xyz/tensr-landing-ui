---
title: McNemar test
description: Test a change in a binary measure on the same cases.
category: Categorical
---

## When to use it

Use McNemar when the same people are measured twice on a yes/no variable. Only the people who changed contribute to the test. For example: did more people pass after a workshop than before it?

## Assumptions

Each row is one pair. Both columns are binary. Concordant pairs (no change) do not enter the statistic.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Crosstabs. In chat: “McNemar test of before by after.”</Step>
<Step>Column A is the first measure and column B is the second.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Eight pairs. Three changed from no to yes, and one changed from yes to no.

| n pairs | no → yes | yes → no | Statistic | p |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 8 | 3 | 1 | 1.000 | .625 |

p = .625 is above .05. With only four discordant pairs, the test does not show a change.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A McNemar test found no significant change from before to after, p = .63. Three pairs changed from no to yes and one changed from yes to no.

## Coming from SPSS

Crosstabs → Statistics → McNemar. The menu label is still on the Analyze menu even though an older retired-label list still names McNemar.
