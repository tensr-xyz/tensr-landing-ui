## When to use it

Use mixed ANOVA when some groups are separate people and another factor is the same people measured more than once. For example: do control and treatment groups differ in how their scores change from pre to post?

The interaction is usually the result you care about. It asks whether the pre-to-post change is different in the two groups.

## Assumptions

Between groups, the spread of scores should be similar. Within people, sphericity applies when there are three or more repeated measurements. With only two repeated measurements, sphericity holds automatically, and Mauchly’s test is not informative.

With only two repeated columns, Tensr does not print Mauchly’s test. It does not run Levene’s test or Shapiro–Wilk. Partial η² is printed for each effect. You do not choose it.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → General Linear Model → Mixed ANOVA. In chat: “Mixed ANOVA of pre and post by group, with id as the subject.”</Step>
<Step>Subject column identifies the person. Between factor is the grouping column. Within measures are the repeated columns, at least two, on a single row per person.</Step>
<Step>Confidence level starts at 0.95. There is no post-hoc option and no switch for sphericity.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Eight people, four Control and four Treatment, with `pre` and `post` columns. Control stays near 5. Treatment moves from about 5 to about 8.

Between-subjects effects are the group comparison, using each person’s average of pre and post. Within-subjects effects are the repeated factor (pre versus post) and the group × condition interaction. Partial η² is that effect’s sum of squares divided by the effect plus its error. With two within levels, Mauchly’s test is not printed.

Group × condition means show the pattern the interaction tests.

Between-subjects effects:

| Source | SS | df | MS | F | p | partial η² |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Between: group | 6.25 | 1 | 6.25 | 5.769 | .053 | 0.49 |
| Error | 6.5 | 6 | 1.083 | — | — | — |

Within-subjects effects:

| Source | SS | df | F | p | partial η² |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| condition | 12.25 | 1 | 147 | < .001 | 0.961 |
| group × condition | 6.25 | 1 | 75 | < .001 | 0.926 |

Group × condition means:

| Group | post | pre |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Control | 5.5 | 5 |
| Treatment | 8 | 5 |

The group main effect is not significant at .05 (p = .053). Scores rise from pre to post, and that rise is larger in the treatment group.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A mixed ANOVA tested pre and post scores in control and treatment groups (n = 4 per group). The group effect was not significant, F(1, 6) = 5.77, p = .053, partial η² = .49. Scores changed from pre to post, F = 147, p < .001, partial η² = .96. The group × condition interaction was significant, F = 75, p < .001, partial η² = .93. The within table does not print an error row, so those two F ratios are quoted without a second degrees of freedom. Control means were 5.00 at pre and 5.50 at post. Treatment means were 5.00 at pre and 8.00 at post.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → General Linear Model → Repeated Measures, with a between-subjects factor.

SPSS prints the interaction F, its degrees of freedom, and partial η² in the within-subjects table.

## Related
