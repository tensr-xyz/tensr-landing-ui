title: Goodman–Kruskal lambda
description: How much knowing one category reduces errors about another.
category: Categorical
---

## When to use it

Use lambda when both variables are categories, ordered or not, and you want the reduction in prediction errors. Lambda is 0 when the extra variable does not help, and 1 when it predicts the other variable perfectly.

## Assumptions

Both columns are categorical. Lambda can be zero even when chi-square is significant, because it asks a stricter prediction question.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Correlate → Lambda. In chat: “Lambda for dose and response.”</Step>
<Step>Column A and column B are the two categorical variables.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Same ten-case table.

| Symmetric λ | A dependent | B dependent | SE | z | p |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0.500 | 0.571 | 0.429 | 0.158 | 3.162 | .002 |

Symmetric lambda is .50, p = .002. Knowing one variable cuts prediction errors about in half on this tiny table. With only ten cases, treat the size as fragile even though the p-value is small.

## Reporting (APA 7)
