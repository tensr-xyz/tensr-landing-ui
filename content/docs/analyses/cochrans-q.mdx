## When to use it

Use Cochran’s Q when the same people are measured three or more times on a yes/no variable. It is the binary cousin of the Friedman test. McNemar is the two-occasion version.

## Assumptions

Each column is a repeated binary measure. Values are treated as success or failure. You need at least three columns.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Nonparametric Tests → Cochran. In chat: “Cochran’s Q for these binary columns.”</Step>
<Step>Select at least three measure columns.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Eight people and three binary items. Success counts were 5, 3, and 3.

| Q | df | p |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2.000 | 2 | .368 |

p = .368. The success rates do not differ on this sample.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Cochran’s Q did not show a difference across the three binary items, Q(2, N = 8) = 2.00, p = .37.
