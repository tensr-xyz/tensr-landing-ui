## When to use it

Use this when both variables are categories and you want to know whether the pattern of counts could have come from independence. For example: does passing a quiz depend on teaching method?

## Assumptions

Each row is one case. Expected counts should be at least 5 in most cells. Tensr stores `low_expected_cells`, the number of cells whose expected count is under 5. When that number is high, use [Fisher’s exact test](/docs/analyses/fishers-exact) on a 2×2 table.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Crosstabs. In chat: “Chi-square of method by passed.”</Step>
<Step>Column A and column B are the two categorical variables. Order does not change the chi-square.</Step>
<Step>Phi and Cramér’s V are on by default. Fisher’s exact is off. Turning it on replaces this test with Fisher’s exact.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Sixty people. `group` is exposed or unexposed. `outcome` is event or none. Counts are exposed 22 event / 8 none, unexposed 12 event / 18 none. Expected counts are 17 and 13 in each row. `low_expected_cells` is 0.

| χ² | df | p | Phi | Cramér’s V |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5.498 | 1 | .019 | 0.303 | 0.303 |

p = .019 is below .05, so the outcome is associated with group. Phi and Cramér’s V are the same on a 2×2 table. Around .30 is a moderate association.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A chi-square test of independence showed that outcome and group were associated, χ²(1, N = 60) = 5.50, p = .019, φ = .30.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Crosstabs, with Chi-square, Phi, and Cramér’s V. SPSS prints expected counts in the crosstab. Tensr stores them on `expected_counts` and counts how many fall under 5.
