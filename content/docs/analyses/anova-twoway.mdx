## When to use it

Use two-way ANOVA when two categorical factors might both affect a numeric outcome, and you care whether they combine. For example: do exam scores depend on teaching method, on morning versus afternoon, and on the combination of the two?

A significant interaction means the teaching gap is not the same in the morning as in the afternoon. You then describe the pattern in each combination, not just the two averages.

## Assumptions

Scores are numeric and independent. Cell sizes should be adequate, and the spread of scores should be similar across cells.

Tensr does not run Levene’s test or Shapiro–Wilk for this procedure. There is no effect-size option. The featured metric is R², the proportion of outcome variance the full model accounts for. If you need a homogeneity check, it is not on this page.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → General Linear Model → Two-Way ANOVA. In chat: “Two-way ANOVA of score by teaching and time.”</Step>
<Step>Factor A and factor B are the two grouping columns. Value column is the numeric outcome.</Step>
<Step>Include interaction starts on. Post hoc starts at none, with the same choices as one-way ANOVA: tukey, bonferroni, scheffe, games_howell.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Twenty-four scores, crossed by teaching (Lecture, Workshop) and time (Morning, Afternoon). Interaction was on. Post hoc was Tukey. The sums of squares are Type II.

| Source | Sum of squares | df | F | p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| teaching | 988.167 | 1 | 93.962 | < .001 |
| time | 4.167 | 1 | 0.396 | .536 |
| teaching × time | 10.667 | 1 | 1.014 | .326 |
| Residual | 210.333 | 20 | — | — |

Teaching is significant: the methods differ, averaging over time of day. Time is not: morning and afternoon means are close. The interaction p of .326 means you do not have evidence that the teaching gap changes with time of day. R² is 0.827, so this model accounts for most of the variation in the toy scores.

p < .05 is the significance line. Residual has no F, because it is the error term the other F ratios are divided by.

Tukey compared the two teaching methods. The star sits in its own column. Time of day is not in this pairwise table.

| Group A | Group B | Mean difference | p (adj) | * | CI low | CI high | Reject H₀ |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Lecture | Workshop | 12.833 | < .001 | *** | 10.125 | 15.542 | True |

The chart is a box plot of score by teaching (factor A only).

## Reporting (APA 7)

A two-way ANOVA tested exam scores by teaching method and time of day (N = 24). The teaching main effect was significant, F(1, 20) = 93.96, p < .001. The time-of-day main effect was not, F(1, 20) = 0.40, p = .536, and the teaching × time interaction was not, F(1, 20) = 1.01, p = .326. The model R² was .83. Workshop scores were higher than lecture scores, mean difference = 12.83, 95% CI [10.13, 15.54], p < .001.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → General Linear Model → Univariate.

SPSS Univariate can print partial η², estimated marginal means, and Levene’s test from the Options button. Tensr’s table has F and p, plus R² as a metric, and does not print partial η² or a homogeneity test. ANCOVA in Tensr is a separate menu item, not a covariate you add inside this dialog.
