## When to use it

Use the Mantel–Haenszel trend test when groups are ordered and the outcome is binary. It asks whether the outcome rate rises or falls across that order, instead of treating the groups as unordered the way chi-square does.

## Assumptions

The grouping column should sort into the order you mean. Numeric-looking labels sort numerically. Other labels sort alphabetically.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Correlate → Mantel-Haenszel Trend. In chat: “Mantel–Haenszel trend of dose by illness.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is the ordered exposure. Outcome column is binary.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Twelve people, dose groups 1, 2, and 3, outcome ill or not. Illness becomes more common as the dose label increases.

| χ² | df | p | Trend coefficient |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 2.000 | 1 | .157 | 0.250 |

p = .157. The trend is in the expected direction, and this sample is too small to call it significant.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A Mantel–Haenszel trend test did not show a significant increase in illness across dose, χ²(1, N = 12) = 2.00, p = .16.
