## When to use it

Use Poisson regression when the outcome is a count: absences, errors, visits. The slope is on the log-count scale. The incidence rate ratio (IRR) says how many times larger the expected count is for a one-unit increase in the predictor.

## Assumptions

Counts are non-negative. The mean and the variance of the count are similar. If the variance is much larger than the mean, a [negative binomial regression](/docs/analyses/negative-binomial-regression) is the usual next model. Tensr does not choose that for you.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Regression → Poisson. In chat: “Poisson regression of absences on anxiety.”</Step>
<Step>Dependent is the count. Independents need at least one predictor.</Step>
<Step>Confidence level starts at 0.95. Include constant starts on.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Absence counts generated as Poisson, with anxiety raising the mean a little. These counts are not the over-dispersed visit counts.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Regression → Poisson.

SPSS Generalized Linear Models is the usual menu for a Poisson log link. Tensr’s menu path is Analyze → Regression → Poisson.
