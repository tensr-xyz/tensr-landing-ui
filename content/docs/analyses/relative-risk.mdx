---
title: Relative risk
description: Risk of a binary outcome in one group divided by the risk in another.
category: Categorical
---

## When to use it

Use relative risk when you have a 2×2 table and you want a ratio of probabilities, not odds. The first alphabetical outcome level is the event. The first group level is the exposed group.

## Assumptions

Both columns have exactly two levels. Relative risk is easiest to interpret when the rows are groups and the columns are the outcome of a cohort, not a case-control sample.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Relative Risk. In chat: “Relative risk of group by outcome.”</Step>
<Step>Check alphabetical order before you read `risk_exposed`.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Same 60-person table. Risk of `event` is 0.733 in `exposed` and 0.400 in `unexposed`.

| Relative risk | 95% CI | p |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1.833 | [1.125, 2.988] | .018 |

The event is about 1.8 times as common in the exposed group. `nnt` is empty on this run because the relative risk is above 1. Tensr only fills NNT when the relative risk is below 1.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Exposed participants were more likely to have the event than unexposed participants, RR = 1.83, 95% CI [1.12, 2.99], p = .018.
