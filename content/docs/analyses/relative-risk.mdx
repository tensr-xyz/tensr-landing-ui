---
title: Relative risk
description: Risk of a binary outcome in one group divided by the risk in another.
category: Categorical
---

## When to use it

Use relative risk when you have a 2×2 table and you want a ratio of probabilities, not odds. The first alphabetical outcome level is the event. The first group level is the exposed group.

## Assumptions

Both columns have exactly two levels. Relative risk is easiest to interpret when the rows are groups and the columns are the outcome of a cohort, not a case-control sample.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Relative Risk. In chat: “Relative risk of group by outcome.”</Step>
<Step>Check alphabetical order before you read `risk_exposed`.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

The same weak 2×2, as a ratio of risks rather than odds.

| exposed | No | Yes |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| No | 28 | 15 |
| Yes | 23 | 24 |

Relative risk of `case` by `exposed`, n = 90. The primary result is not significant (p = .140). This is not large enough to treat the comparison this page is about as a reliable association. The result is non-significant: the data are still compatible with no effect. Metrics: Relative risk = 1.331; 95% CI = [0.924, 1.917]; p-value = .140; Risk (exposed) = 0.651; Risk (unexposed) = 0.489.

Relative risk of `case` by `exposed`, n = 90. This result is not significant (p = .140). Report the estimate with that p, and do not describe the pattern as a reliable effect.
