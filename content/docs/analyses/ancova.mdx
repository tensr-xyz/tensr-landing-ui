## When to use it

Use ANCOVA when groups might differ on an outcome partly because they already differed on something you measured first. For example: do lecture and workshop students differ on the exam after you account for their pretest?

The covariate should be related to the outcome and measured before the grouping, or at least not caused by it. ANCOVA does not fix a badly designed study. It adjusts the group comparison for a linear relationship with the covariate.

## Assumptions

The outcome and the covariate are numeric. The groups are separate. The slope that links the covariate to the outcome should be roughly the same in every group. That is homogeneity of regression slopes.

Tensr does not test that assumption for you. The option `include_interaction` fits a group × covariate term, which is the check, and it starts off. Turn it on if you need to see whether the slopes differ. If that term is significant, a single adjusted group difference is hard to defend.

There is no Levene test, no Shapiro–Wilk test, and no partial η² column. The featured metric is R².

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → General Linear Model → ANCOVA. In chat: “ANCOVA of score by method, controlling for pretest.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is the grouping variable. Outcome column is the numeric result. Covariate column is the numeric control.</Step>
<Step>Include interaction starts off, so the default model is group plus covariate, without a product term.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Twelve students, six Lecture and six Workshop, with a pretest and an exam score. Interaction was left off. Sums of squares are Type II. R² was 0.998 because the toy scores are almost a straight line of the pretest plus a method gap. That R² will not show up in a real exam.

| Source | Sum of squares | df | F | p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| method | 477.331 | 1 | 4000 | < .001 |
| pretest | 103.093 | 1 | 863.884 | < .001 |
| Residual | 1.074 | 9 | — | — |

The method row is the group difference after the linear pretest relationship is in the model. p < .001 means the adjusted means differ. The pretest row means the covariate predicts the exam, which is what you want from a covariate. F for method is printed as 4000 because values of 1000 or more use a short format. The unrounded F was about 3999.88.

There is no table of adjusted means. The report summary names the outcome, the group, and the covariate, and gives N.

## Reporting (APA 7)

An ANCOVA compared exam scores for lecture and workshop students, with pretest as the covariate (N = 12). Pretest was a significant covariate, F(1, 9) = 863.88, p < .001. The teaching-method effect, adjusted for pretest, was also significant, F(1, 9) = 3999.88, p < .001. Model R² = .998.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → General Linear Model → Univariate, with the group as a fixed factor and the pretest as a covariate.

SPSS can print estimated marginal means and a homogeneity-of-slopes test if you add the interaction. Tensr’s default leaves that interaction off. Turn on `include_interaction` for the slopes check. Tensr does not print adjusted means.
