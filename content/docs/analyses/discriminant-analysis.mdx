## When to use it

Use discriminant analysis when the outcome is a group and the predictors are numeric. For example: can recall and confidence tell lecture students from workshop students? Logistic regression answers a similar question for two groups. Discriminant analysis reports a function, group centroids, and a classification table.

## Assumptions

Predictors are numeric. The groups have similar covariance. Tensr does not test that. With two groups there is one discriminant function.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Classify → Discriminant. In chat: “Discriminant analysis of method from recall and confidence.”</Step>
<Step>Group column is the category. Columns are the numeric predictors. At least one predictor is required.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Twenty-four students, 12 Lecture and 12 Workshop. Predictors `recall` and `confidence`.

Metrics: classification accuracy 91.7%, groups 2, cases 24, chance accuracy 50%. Chance accuracy is the rate from always guessing the largest group. Here the groups are equal, so chance is 50%. 91.7% is above that.

Group centroids on the discriminant function:

| Group | LD1 |
| --- | ---: |
| Lecture | -1.442 |
| Workshop | 1.442 |

The groups sit on opposite sides of the function. Wilks’ lambda for that function:

| Functions | Wilks' λ | χ² | df | p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.325 | 25.118 | 2 | < .001 |

p < .001 means the predictors separate the groups beyond chance in this sample.

Classification results:

| Actual | Predicted Lecture | Predicted Workshop | % Correct |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lecture | 10 | 2 | 83.3% |
| Workshop | 0 | 12 | 100.0% |

Two lecture students were classified as workshop. Every workshop student was classified correctly.

Coefficients:

| Variable | Coefficient |
| --- | ---: |
| recall | -0.498 |
| confidence | 3.555 |

Confidence has the larger weight, so it does more of the separating on this function.

## Reporting (APA 7)

A discriminant analysis predicted teaching method from recall and confidence (N = 24). The function was significant, Wilks’ Λ = .33, χ²(2) = 25.12, p < .001. Classification accuracy was 91.7%, against a chance rate of 50%.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Classify → Discriminant.

SPSS prints eigenvalues, Wilks’ lambda, standardized coefficients, and a classification table. Tensr prints centroids, Wilks’ lambda, the classification counts, and the coefficients.
