## When to use it

Use Kendall’s W when several judges rank the same items, or several repeated scores should move together. Friedman’s test is the related hypothesis test. W is the effect size, from 0 (no agreement) to 1 (complete agreement).

## Assumptions

Each column is one ranking or one repeated measure. Rows are the items or the people, depending on how you laid the data out. On this procedure Tensr treats columns as judges and rows as items.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Scale → Kendall W. In chat: “Kendall’s W for these columns.”</Step>
<Step>Select at least two numeric columns.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Four items and three judges. Scores rise from the first column to the third.

| W | χ² | df | p |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0.700 | 6.300 | 3 | .098 |

p = .098 is above .05, so this small set does not show significant concordance. W = 0.700 is large on the shared scale used for Kendall’s W and for Friedman’s W: below 0.1 is negligible, 0.1 ≤ W < 0.3 is small, 0.3 ≤ W < 0.5 is moderate, and W ≥ 0.5 is large. Cutoffs: Tomczak & Tomczak, 2014.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Kendall’s W was .70, χ²(3, N = 4) = 6.30, p = .098.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Scale → Kendall’s W. Friedman’s ANOVA is the related nonparametric test when the rows are people and the columns are repeated measures.
