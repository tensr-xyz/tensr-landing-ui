## When to use it

Use gamma when both variables are ordered categories and you want to know whether higher scores on one tend to come with higher scores on the other. Gamma runs from −1 to 1. It ignores ties.

## Assumptions

Both columns are ordered. Tensr sorts level names alphabetically, so the names need to sort in the true order.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Correlate → Gamma. In chat: “Gamma for dose and response.”</Step>
<Step>Column A and column B are the two ordered variables.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Ten cases. Dose is low, mid, or high. Response is none, mild, or marked. Alphabetical order is not the clinical order for these words, so read the sign against the sorted labels, not against low-to-high.

| Gamma | SE | z | p | Concordant | Discordant |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0.182 | 0.127 | 1.430 | .153 | 13 | 9 |

p = .153. The association is small and not significant on this sample.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Goodman–Kruskal gamma was .18, p = .15.
