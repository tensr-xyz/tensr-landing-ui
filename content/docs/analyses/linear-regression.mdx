## When to use it

Use linear regression when you want an equation: how many exam points go with one extra study hour, holding anxiety constant? Correlation only says the columns move together. Regression gives the slope.

## Assumptions

The outcome is numeric. The straight-line model is a fair summary, the spread of residuals is roughly even, and the rows are independent. Residual plots and collinearity diagnostics are how Tensr shows those checks. They are not a formal hypothesis test of the assumptions.

## Running it in Tensr

<Steps>
<Step>Analyze → Regression → Linear. In chat: “Regress score on hours and anxiety.”</Step>
<Step>Dependent is the outcome. Independents are the predictors. At least one predictor is required.</Step>
<Step>Method starts at enter, which puts every predictor in at once. Confidence level starts at 0.95. Include constant starts on.</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading the output

Same 24 students. Outcome `score`. Predictors `hours` and `anxiety`. Method enter.

R² is 0.988 and adjusted R² is 0.987. N is 24. The model F p-value is < .001, so the predictors together are not a plausible zero.

| Term | Estimate | p-value | 95% CI low | 95% CI high |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Intercept | 67.73 | < .001 | 60.51 | 74.95 |
| hours | 3.367 | < .001 | 2.644 | 4.09 |
| anxiety | -1.97 | < .001 | -2.618 | -1.323 |

The hours slope is the exam-point change for one extra hour, holding anxiety constant. The anxiety slope is the point change for one extra anxiety point, holding hours constant. Both intervals exclude 0.

Diagnostics on this run: RMSE 1.105, Durbin–Watson 1.745, Jarque–Bera statistic 0.85, Jarque–Bera p .654, residual skewness 0.46, residual kurtosis 3.052. Jarque–Bera p .654 does not flag a badly skewed residual in this toy set.

Because there are two predictors, collinearity diagnostics were on:

| Variable | VIF | Tolerance |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| hours | 7.008 | 0.143 |
| anxiety | 7.008 | 0.143 |

VIF above about 5 means the predictors overlap a lot. Hours and anxiety are strongly related in this example, so each slope is harder to separate from the other. Tolerance is 1 / VIF.

## Stepwise regression

Stepwise is a Method value on this page, not a separate analysis. Forward adds the predictor that helps most, one step at a time. Backward starts with all of them and drops the weakest. Stepwise can add and later remove. Enter ignores that search and keeps every predictor.

The same 24 rows with `method: "stepwise"`:

| Step | Variable entered/removed | R² | ΔR² | F change | p |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | entered: hours | 0.965 | 0.965 | 610.235 | < .001 |
| 2 | entered: anxiety | 0.988 | 0.023 | 40.07 | < .001 |

Hours entered first. Anxiety still added a small slice of R². The final coefficients matched the enter model above, because both predictors stayed in.

## Reporting (APA 7)

Exam score was regressed on study hours and anxiety (N = 24). The model was significant, p < .001, R² = .99, adjusted R² = .99. Each extra study hour was associated with 3.37 points, 95% CI [2.64, 4.09], p < .001, and each extra anxiety point with −1.97 points, 95% CI [−2.62, −1.32], p < .001.

## Coming from SPSS

Analyze → Regression → Linear.

SPSS Method offers Enter, Stepwise, Remove, Backward, and Forward. Tensr’s `method` values are enter, stepwise, backward, and forward. SPSS Statistics also offers collinearity and plots from the same dialog. Tensr’s switches are `collinearity_diagnostics` and `residual_plots`.
